- Revenue of EUR 184.6 million, up 7.8% on Q3 2025. Teklund Power Electronics, acquired

Industrial Sensors. Revenue fell 6.2%. Demand from machine builders in Germany and Italy
stayed weak, and we cut list prices by about 4% on our pressure sensor range to answer
lower-cost competitors from China. Volumes held up better than prices. The segment's
largest customer, Vessmann Automotive, accounted for 14% of group revenue in the first
nine months.

Medical Devices. Revenue grew 7.3% on strong demand for patient-monitoring sensors in
North America. Production at the Lund plant stopped for nine days in August after a
ransomware attack on a component supplier; the disruption cost about EUR 1.4 million,
booked in cost of sales. Margin still improved thanks to a better product mix.

Energy Systems. Includes Teklund from 1 July. Excluding Teklund, segment revenue was
EUR 13.7 million, down from EUR 17.2 million, as two utility customers postponed grid
projects into 2027. Teklund's own margin was lower than planned (see risks).

Corporate and other includes the Ostrava gain (EUR +5.9 million), acquisition-related
costs (EUR -3.1 million) and central costs (EUR -3.3 million; Q3 2025: EUR -3.6 million).

Financing. The Teklund acquisition was funded with cash and a new EUR 80 million bridge
loan, which matures in June 2027. Our revolving credit facility carries one covenant:
net debt may not exceed 2.5 times adjusted EBITDA over the last twelve months. Adjusted
EBITDA for the last twelve months, including Teklund on a pro forma basis, was
EUR 118.0 million at 30 September.


5. OUTLOOK

For the full year 2026 we now expect revenue of EUR 720-740 million and an adjusted EBIT
margin of 13-14%. The higher revenue range reflects Teklund. The lower margin range
reflects price pressure in Industrial Sensors, the Teklund margin and a weaker US dollar:
about 38% of group revenue is invoiced in US dollars, and the dollar was on average 6%
weaker against the euro than a year ago.


6. PRINCIPAL RISKS

a) Customer concentration. Vessmann Automotive told us in September that it will qualify
   a second supplier for its 2027 vehicle platforms. Vessmann has not said how much
   volume it will move. Our contract with Vessmann runs to December 2027 with no minimum
   purchase commitment.

b) Teklund product quality. Shortly after closing we found a firmware defect in about
   2,400 Teklund inverters already installed at customer sites. A provision of
   EUR 2.2 million was recorded in the purchase accounting. Our current estimate of the
   total cost of a field fix ranges from EUR 2 million to EUR 7 million, depending on how
   many units need a site visit rather than a remote update.

c) Price competition in Industrial Sensors. Further price cuts may be needed in 2027 if
   competitors keep expanding in Europe. Each 1% of price on the segment's revenue is
   worth about EUR 4 million a year in EBIT.

d) Working capital and liquidity. Receivables and inventories rose faster than revenue
   during the quarter, partly because Teklund's customers pay on longer terms. Operating
   cash flow fell to EUR 9.8 million.

e) Refinancing. The EUR 80 million bridge loan must be refinanced or repaid by June 2027.
   We plan to replace it with a longer-term loan in the first half of 2027.

f) Supply chain and cyber security. The August incident showed that a single supplier
   outage can stop a plant. We are qualifying second sources for four critical
   components and have asked our top 30 suppliers for evidence of their security controls.

g) Currency. A further 5% fall of the US dollar against the euro would reduce annual
   revenue by about EUR 14 million and EBIT by about EUR 3 million.
